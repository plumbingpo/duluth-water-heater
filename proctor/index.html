Keeping Your Proctor Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
