How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

Consider the vent first. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the new heater to your household, confirm the gas line, vent, and electrical service can support it, and install every code item your home requires. We leak-test, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature before we leave. That is the difference between a heater that runs quietly for a decade and one that starts with a problem on day one. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
