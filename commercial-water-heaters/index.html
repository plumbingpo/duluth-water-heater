Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Scalding bursts or wild temperature swings at showers or sinks
  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank

A commercial heater is not just a large residential one. Higher gas input means the supply line, meter, combustion air, and vent all have to be sized for the load. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Record fixture temperatures regularly, especially before inspections
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
