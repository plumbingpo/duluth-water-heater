Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals drop out of solution and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Duluth, MN, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Duluth Water Heater at (218) 276-4521 for a prompt inspection.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Maintenance?

Homeowners can handle the basics: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest carries more risk than it looks. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not a place to guess.

Our trucks are stocked for the usual surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring experience. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, tell us where a heater is headed, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
