How Rice Lake Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
