Keeping Your Two Harbors Water Heater Running Longer

Many early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Two Harbors

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them around Two Harbors. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
